# Categories

**Phase:** Consolidation (Part 2 — Fan In + Home In)
**Status:** Essential. Per Part 2 slide 25 speaker notes: _"Two of them are flagged as ESSENTIAL: Categories and Weighted Metrics."_

**Source — training narrative:** Design Part 2 deck slides 28–35 (intro, three approaches, where-to-find-it); slide 50 (live practice instruction text); slide 56 (debrief).
**Source — canonical activity card:** Menu of Design Activities slide 41.
**Source assets (ideas42 internal):** Starter Deck; Input Sheet Template. Both linked from the activity card on Menu slide 41. Pending pull into `04_Assets/design-activities/`.
**Design Guide point person:** Katy Davis.
**Function (per Menu):** Organize ideas. _"Methodically organize the chaotic output of generation sessions by separating ideas by category: products, components of products, or overarching framing."_
**Effort (per Menu):** 2–6 hours. 1–2 people (Design Lead + optional helper).

## Purpose

A first-pass consolidation activity. Takes the raw output of ideation (typically 100–400 ideas) and clusters them into thematic groups so the team can see the shape of the idea space and find duplicates / gaps.

## When to use it

- **First consolidation, default opener.** Per Part 2 slide 28 speaker notes: _"Now that all the ideas are organized, we highly recommend the first activity, CATEGORIES, to just organize them into broader categories."_
- **Useful in BOTH consolidation moments** — Menu slide 41: _"Categories is useful after any messy, wonderful generation session (or sessions). It's equally useful in (2) Fan In and (4) Home In."_
- **Iterative** — Part 2 slide 28 notes: _"In fact, you will probably use the categories activity more than once."_
- Skip only if your ideation output is small (<30 ideas).

## How it differs from neighbors

- vs **Dot Voting**: Categories *organizes* without prioritizing. Dot Voting *prioritizes* without organizing. They typically run in sequence (Categories first, then Dot Voting on the categorized output).
- vs **Weighted Metrics**: Weighted Metrics is for second-consolidation (narrowing 20–50 ideas to 4–10) using explicit criteria. Categories is upstream of that.
- vs **MoSCoW**: MoSCoW classifies by must/should/could/won't — that's a partner-engagement framing, not a clustering framing.

## Three approaches (per Part 2 slides 30–32)

1. **Emergent** (slide 30) — _"free form tagging each idea and see which categories emerge."_ Best when the team has no prior frame on the idea space.
2. **Pre-defined** (slide 31) — start with one of the canonical category sets the trainings call out:
   - **Level** — big ideas vs components vs design principles
   - **Diagnosis** — by behavioral barrier or contextual feature (the most ideas42-aligned cut)
   - **Channel** — in-person, at home, digital, etc.
   - **Perspective** — student, teacher, family member, administrator, etc.
   - **Process / stage** — at which point in the user journey the idea applies
3. **Hybrid / custom** (slide 32) — _"create your own!"_ Start emergent; layer in a canonical set to round out gaps.

**Caution from Part 2 slide 29: "ORGANIZING REQUIRES JUDGMENT."** First step (per speaker notes) is to **remove duplicates and clarify ideas into a Shortlist of no more than 20** — _"this can vary based on your project goals, but no more than 20"_ is the canonical recommendation. Categories are not neutral; the framing shapes which ideas survive. Surface this trade-off explicitly.

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Pull the raw idea output from ideation (typically 100–400 ideas) into a single one-idea-per-row sheet or sticky-note board.
- [ ] **First, dedupe and shortlist** — Part 2 slide 29 speaker notes are explicit: remove duplicates, clarify ambiguous ideas, narrow to a shortlist of no more than 20 _before_ categorizing.
- [ ] Decide approach (emergent / pre-defined / hybrid). Default is hybrid for ideas42 projects — start emergent for 15 min, then check against the Diagnosis cut.
- [ ] Pick the tool: virtual board (Miro, FigJam, Trello — Trello called out as a Portland co-design example on Part 2 slide 34) or in-person sticky notes. Both work; Menu slide 41 confirms Categories is fine for either.
- [ ] Pull the Starter Deck once `04_Assets/design-activities/categories-Starter-Deck.pptx` is available; use the Input Sheet Template structure to set up the working board.
- [ ] Block 2–6 hours total (per Menu); typically split across 2 sessions.

## In-session timing (typical, ~2 hours)

- 5 min: framing — what we're doing and why; remind team of the design challenge
- 20 min: dedupe pass — remove obvious duplicates, clarify vague entries
- 5 min: agree on shortlist (≤20 ideas) — hand off the full list to the parking lot
- 30–45 min: cluster the shortlisted ideas (silent first per Part 2 slide 34 speaker notes; "making it a silent activity can help equalize the group members"; then discuss edge cases as a group)
- 10 min: name the categories
- 10 min: spot gaps — Part 2 slide 33 explicitly raises _"Do you need more ideation?"_ as the post-Categories checkpoint
- 5 min: capture, snapshot, hand off

## Prompt templates

The 10-min training-practice version (Part 2 slide 50), verbatim:

> **Categories (10 min):**
> _In our breakout rooms, we will visit the design training input sheet to:_
> _- Observe categories that naturally arise (as a group),_
> _- Consider categories (as a group), and_
> _- Assign categories (individually)._

The full design-phase version, parameterized:

> **Categories — for {design-challenge}**
> _We have {idea-count} ideas from {ideation-activities}. Today we'll narrow to a shortlist of ~20 and group them so we can see the shape of the idea space._
>
> _Step 1 ({duplication-time}, individual): Scan the full list. Flag any duplicates with `[DUP]` and any ambiguous ideas with `[?]`. Don't categorize yet — just clean._
>
> _Step 2 ({shortlist-time}, group): Walk the flagged list. Resolve the `[?]`s. Pick the 20 ideas that survive — the rest go to the parking lot, not the trash._
>
> _Step 3 ({categorize-time}, silent → group): Looking at the shortlist, tag each idea with the {category-cut} (e.g., behavioral barrier, channel, level). Tag silently first; we'll merge tags as a group after._
>
> _Step 4: Discuss — which category surprised us? Which is thin and might need more ideation?_

## Common pitfalls

- Skipping the dedupe-and-shortlist pass and going straight to categorization. The training is explicit that this is _the_ first step (Part 2 slide 29). Categorizing 200 raw ideas is a waste — categorize the 20 that survive cleanup.
- Too many categories (>10) — you've under-clustered. Push to merge.
- Too few categories (<3) — you've over-clustered. Push to split.
- Letting the loudest voice anchor the category labels first. Use silent clustering before group naming (Part 2 slide 34 speaker notes call this out for "someone with a strong personality who tends to monopolize").
- Skipping the gap check (Part 2 slide 33) — if a category has 2 ideas while another has 40, the team may have a whitespace area worth more ideation.

## Debrief questions (training-standard, Part 2 slide 56)

- How did it go?
- What came up in your break-out room discussion?
- How have people consolidated ideas on past projects? What problems did you have?
- How might you adapt these activities to a past project or a future project?

Project-specific debrief additions:

- Which category surprised the team?
- Which is thinnest? Do we need more ideation there before continuing to second consolidation?
- Are any categories actually redundant?
- Which ideas refuse to fit anywhere — and what does that tell us?

## Output handoff

The categorized shortlist typically feeds:

1. **Dot Voting** next, if the team needs lightweight prioritization (especially with partners).
2. **Weighted Metrics** directly, if the team has explicit criteria and is ready for second consolidation.
3. **More ideation** (back to Change the Context / Forced Fitting / Trade) if the gap check (Part 2 slide 33) reveals thin categories.

Don't skip the gap check.
