**Function (per Menu):** Sketch multiple versions of a concept, discuss, then synthesize individually.
**Effort (per Menu):** 45 min – 2 hours. 1–10+ people. Good for remote teams AND co-design (one of few activities flagged for both).

## Purpose

A rapid, time-boxed group sketching activity. Each participant takes a single shortlisted idea and produces a rough pen-and-paper sketch of what the design might look like in practice. The point is to externalize ideas into a tangible form fast, before committing real production effort.

## When to use it

- **After second consolidation**, once you have 4–10 elaborated ideas and need to start visualizing them.
- **Before Assumption Listing**, because the act of sketching surfaces hidden assumptions that the conversation didn't.
- **Optional pre-step before Elaboration** — per the Simple Instructions doc, if your team has time, run a group Charrette first and then designate one "idea owner" who consolidates and decides on the final elaboration.

## How it differs from neighbors

- vs **B-Mapping Lite**: Charrette produces a sketch of the design *itself*; B-Mapping Lite produces a sketch of the *user's behavioral journey* through the design. Often run as a pair.
- vs **Elaboration**: Elaboration is one-person, deep, written. Charrette is group, fast, visual.

## Prep checklist

- [ ] Have a shortlist of "Big Ideas" to sketch — typically 4–10 ideas after second consolidation. The Big Idea is the unit; components are sketched within it.
- [ ] **Pre-fill the One-Pager Specifications doc** for each Big Idea (or have groups do it as Step 1 of the Charrette — see practice script below). The One-Pager template structure is fixed (Part 3 slide 56):
  - Big Idea (the headline)
  - Driving Behavioral Insights
  - Potential Components
  - Questions to Answer via Design Charrette
- [ ] Materials: paper, pens/sharpies, the One-Pager template (pull `04_Assets/design-activities/charrette-One-Pager-Template.docx` from Box once available).
- [ ] Block 30 min – 2 hours per Menu (training compressed example is 30 min; real sessions are usually 60–90 min).
- [ ] Set the rough-sketch constraint up front (Part 3 slide 57: _"Sketching is pretty straightforward. Hardest part is letting yourself sketch roughly."_).
- [ ] Decide layer of sketch: a screen? a moment in the user journey? a flow? a physical artifact? Pick one or label clearly per Big Idea.

## In-session timing (canonical 30-min practice version, Part 3 slide 59)

- 10 min: **One-Pager** — as a group, fill in the One-Pager template for the selected Big Idea (or review it if pre-filled)
- 5 min: **Solo Sketching** — using scratch paper and a pencil, draw 1 version of this idea
- 10 min: **Discussion** — each person 1–2 min: explain what they drew and why. _"Focus on what you were trying to accomplish, what solutions you were excited about, and what problems you weren't able to solve."_ (Part 3 slide 59 verbatim)
- 5 min: capture, hand off

For real (non-training) sessions, scale up: 15–20 min One-Pager, 15–25 min sketching (multiple versions per person if time), 20–30 min share-back, 5–10 min capture.

## The "synthesize" follow-up step

Part 3 slide 58 ends the Charrette by synthesizing sketches into a **design package**. This is typically post-session — the idea-owner takes everyone's sketches + share-back notes and produces one coherent design write-up per Big Idea. Example output structure from slide 58: a 3–4-line description that combines the most promising elements (e.g., reminders + commitment device + temptation bundling + social norm + shared tracker → one running-routine intervention).

## Prompt templates

The 30-min training-practice version (Part 3 slide 59), verbatim:

> **Design Charrette (30 min)**
> _- One Pager [10 minutes]: As a group, you'll create a one-pager of specifications for our selected Big Idea._
> _- Solo Sketching [5 minutes]: Using scratch paper and a pencil, draw 1 version of this idea._
> _- Discussion [10 minutes]: As time permits, each person will have 1-2 minutes to explain what they drew and why. Focus on what you were trying to accomplish, what solutions you were excited about, and what problems you weren't able to solve._

The One-Pager template structure (verbatim from Part 3 slide 56), parameterized:

```
ONE-PAGER: {Big-Idea-name}

Big Idea: {one-sentence description of the intervention}

Driving Behavioral Insights:
- {behavioral insight 1 — which barrier or contextual feature this addresses}
- {behavioral insight 2}
- {behavioral insight 3}

Potential Components:
- {component 1 — a feature, channel, or moment of intervention}
- {component 2}
- {component 3}

Questions to Answer via Design Charrette:
- {question 1 — what specifically should the sketches resolve?}
- {question 2}
```

Sketching guidance (Part 3 slide 57, verbatim):

> _- Get as many ideas on paper._
> _- Don't worry about constraints yet. Sort of like ideation._
> _- Also like ideation: let yourself merge and piggyback on ideas._

Share-back framing prompt (synthesized from slide 59):

> _Each person, 1–2 minutes:_
> _1. What were you trying to accomplish with this version?_
> _2. What solutions were you excited about?_
> _3. What problems weren't you able to solve?_

Synthesis template for the post-session design package (Part 3 slide 58 example):

> _**{Big-Idea-name} — synthesized design package**_
> _Combine the most promising elements from the team's sketches. Aim for 3–5 components that address the driving behavioral insights:_
> _- {component A from sketch 1}_
> _- {component B from sketch 2}_
> _- {component C — synthesized from sketches 3 + 4}_
> _- {component D — emergent, didn't appear in any single sketch}_

## Common pitfalls

- Letting sketches go too high-fidelity. The point is speed and iteration, not polish. Push back on perfectionism — Part 3 slide 57 is explicit: rough.
- Skipping the share-back. Sketches that aren't shared don't generate the cross-pollination that makes the activity worth running.
- Not defining "what gets sketched" up front — UI vs service flow vs physical artifact. Different team members will sketch at different layers and the share-back becomes incoherent.
- Skipping the One-Pager step. Without it, the Charrette becomes "draw whatever you think 'this idea' means" and the sketches diverge so far they don't synthesize.
- Treating the sketch as the final design. It's a starting point. Polish phase exists for a reason (10-step process Step 9).

## Debrief questions (training-standard, Part 3 slide 63)

- How did it go?
- What came up in your break-out room discussion?
- Would you feel comfortable using this activity on a project?

Project-specific debrief additions:

- Whose sketch surprised you?
- Where do the sketches converge — and where do they diverge?
- What did sketching surface that the conversation didn't?
- Which assumptions are baked into each sketch? (Hand off to Assumption Listing — Step 5 of the 10-step doc.)

## Output handoff

The Charrette output feeds:

1. **Assumption Listing** (Simple Instructions Step 5) — sketches make hidden assumptions visible.
2. **B-Mapping Lite** — the synthesized design package becomes the input to the behavioral journey trace.
3. **Prototyping** (Simple Instructions Step 6) — the rough sketches become the basis for higher-fidelity prototypes (PPT, Figma, Balsamiq, Snagit per Simple Instructions).
4. The **design package** synthesis itself (one-page write-up per idea-owner per Part 3 slide 58 example).
