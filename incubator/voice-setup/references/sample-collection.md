# Sample collection

How to coach the user through Phase 2. Goal: get to the floor of 3 samples (ideally 5–7) covering enough register diversity that Phase 3 has real signal to work with.

## Table of contents

## Why register diversity matters

A single sample tells you what the writer sounds like *in that one mode*. Voice across modes is what makes a voice profile actually portable. A person whose Slack messages are sharp and whose blog posts are corporate has two voices, not one — and a voice.md drafted from only the blog posts will produce stiff Slack messages, and vice versa.

Push for samples across **emotional registers** (for people) or **channels** (for brands), not just samples from the same register at different lengths.

## Person — what to ask for

Ask for samples spanning these registers. Aim for at least 3 different ones; 5 is the sweet spot.

| Register | Where to find it | What it surfaces |
|---|---|---|
| Casual | Slack DMs, group texts, casual emails to friends | Default rhythm, conversational tics, contractions |
| Explanatory | Internal docs, how-to emails, project explainers | How they structure ideas, transitions, defining terms |
| Excited | Announcement posts, "I just shipped X" Slack messages, launch emails | Punctuation under enthusiasm, hyperbole tolerance |
| Frustrated / direct | Disagreements, pushback, escalation emails | How they hedge or don't, where they get blunt |
| Persuasive | Pitches, fundraising, applications, manifestos | Long-form structure, opener choices, closer style |

Useful prompts to give the user:

- "Send me 2–3 emails or Slack messages from the last month — pick ones you reread and were happy with."
- "Send me a longer piece you've published or written — blog post, LinkedIn essay, internal memo."
- "If you've ever drafted something twice because the first draft didn't sound like you, send me the second draft."
- "If there's a piece of writing where someone said 'this sounds like you,' send that."

What to **avoid** asking for:
- Drafts the user thinks are "fine" but doesn't love — they'll pull voice toward the average
- Samples ghostwritten by someone else
- Boilerplate (auto-replies, signature blocks, contract language)
- Anything they wrote in a voice they're trying to *escape* (e.g., "I was forced to use corporate-speak in this old job")

## Brand — what to ask for

Brand collection is more directed because brands often have explicit artifacts.

Ask for, in priority order:
1. **Existing style guide / voice charter / brand book** if one exists (PDF, doc, link)
2. **2–3 published pieces** in the brand's flagship channel (whichever they're known for — blog, newsletter, marketing site, product UI copy)
3. **1–2 pieces from a secondary channel** to test cross-channel consistency (e.g., a sales email if the flagship is blog; a LinkedIn post if the flagship is newsletter)
4. **Internal "this is great" reference** — pieces the brand team points to as "this is exactly our voice"

Prompts:

- "Do you have a written style guide, brand book, or voice charter? Even a half-page Google Doc counts."
- "What's the channel where your voice is strongest? Send me 2–3 pieces from there."
- "Send me one thing from a different channel — if blog is your flagship, send a sales email or product page."
- "Is there a piece of writing your team has pointed to and said 'this is the voice'? Send that."

If the brand has no written style guide and the user is drafting this for a small team or solo project, it's normal — proceed with samples alone. Note in voice.md that no style guide existed at capture time.

## Hybrid — person writing as brand

A founder writing as their company. A creator's newsletter. A consultant's client-facing voice.

Ask for samples in **both modes** if both exist:
- The person's pure voice (personal email, casual blog, Slack)
- The person-as-brand voice (newsletter issues, founder updates, client communications)

The voice.md should explicitly note the hybrid: "This is [Name]'s voice when writing as [Brand]. Personal-only voice differs in [X, Y]."

If only one mode exists yet (e.g., founder hasn't published anything as the brand yet), use personal samples and label the voice.md as "first-draft brand voice; revise after first 5 brand publications."

## The redaction check

After uploads, **scan briefly for sensitive content before reading deeply**. Flag if you see:

- Personal: full names paired with addresses / phone / SSN / DOB / financial / medical info, login credentials, private keys
- Brand: unannounced product details, customer names with internal commentary, financial figures the brand hasn't published, legal correspondence
- Either: third-party content the user doesn't have rights to (e.g., a published article they're not the author of)

If flagged, pause and tell the user:

> I noticed some of the samples contain [PII / confidential info / third-party content]. Two options:
> 1. You redact and re-upload — preferred for sensitive material
> 2. I read the samples for voice patterns only and never echo specific values back into voice.md — fine for moderate-sensitivity content
>
> Which do you want?

Default to option 1 for clearly sensitive content (SSNs, medical, financial accounts). Option 2 is fine for things like names and email addresses in casual emails.

Never echo PII from samples back into the voice.md examples. Either rewrite the example with placeholder names and details, or skip it.

## When the user is stuck

Common patterns:

**"I don't have writing samples."**
Almost everyone does. Probe: "When was the last email you wrote that wasn't transactional?" "Do you have a Slack channel where you post longer thoughts?" "Have you written anything you considered publishing but didn't?" If truly nothing, ask them to **write three short samples right now** — a casual update, an explainer for a topic they know, a pitch for something they care about. Use those.

**"All my samples are the same kind of thing."**
This is fine if the user's primary use is also that one thing (e.g., they only want voice for emails — emails-only samples are appropriate). Push for variety only if they want voice that travels across channels.

**"I want my voice to sound like [public figure]."**
Push back gently. This skill captures *their actual voice* from samples; aspiration is a different exercise. You can offer to: (a) capture their current voice now, then (b) note specific patterns from the public figure they want to absorb in a "growth edges" section of voice.md. But the base voice has to be theirs.

**"I want a new voice I don't have yet."**
Same as above — this isn't the right tool. Refer them to a creative writing exercise or a brand strategy session. If they insist, capture their current voice and add a "directional shifts" appendix; they can iterate from there.

## When to hard-stop and request more

Do not start Phase 3 if:
- Fewer than 3 samples
- All samples are under 100 words combined
- All samples are from a single register / channel and the user wants a multi-channel voice
- All samples are from outside the last 2 years (voice drifts over time)
- All samples are forms or boilerplate, not real writing

Tell the user specifically what's missing and what to send. Be concrete: "Send me one longer piece (300+ words) and one casual message, and we're set."
